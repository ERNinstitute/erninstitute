Transparent Article-Level Citation Metrics

Three plain-language metrics answer three questions: How many citations per year is a paper receiving now? Is that annual rate faster or slower than its own lifetime rate? How does it compare with a named annual benchmark?

These are descriptive tools. They make article-level citation claims easier to understand and reproduce, but they do not replace peer review, field-sensitive evaluation, or expert judgment.

One common starting point

All three metrics begin with citations per year. The first reports the annual rate itself. The other two compare that annual rate with another annual rate, so their results are ratios.

The Three Metrics

Now

Citations Per Year (Now)

How many citations per year the paper received during a recent period, usually the past two or five years.

Over time

Citations Per Year (Over Time)

The paper’s recent annual citation rate compared with its own lifetime annual rate.

Named benchmark

Citations Per Year (Against 2y-IF)

The paper’s recent annual citation rate compared with the two-year journal impact factor. If another benchmark is used, name it instead.

1. Citations Per Year (Now)

This is the paper’s recent annual citation rate. Choose a recent period, count the citations received during that period, and average them across the number of years.

Example: A paper received 25 citations during the past 5 years. That is 5 citations per year.

Reported valueWhat it means
0 citations per yearThe paper received no citations during the stated recent period.
1 citation per yearThe paper received one citation per year on average during the stated period.
5 citations per yearThe paper received five citations per year on average; for example, 25 citations over 5 years.

2. Citations Per Year (Over Time)

This compares the paper’s recent citations per year with its lifetime citations per year.

Example: A 10-year-old paper has 100 total citations, so its lifetime rate is 10 citations per year. It received 25 citations during the past 5 years, so its recent rate is 5 citations per year. The reported value is 0.50: the recent rate is half the lifetime rate.

Reported valueWhat it means
Greater than 1.0The paper is being cited faster now than across its lifetime.
About 1.0The recent annual rate is similar to the paper’s lifetime annual rate.
Less than 1.0The paper is being cited more slowly now than across its lifetime.

3. Citations Per Year (Against 2y-IF)

This compares the paper’s recent citations per year with the two-year journal impact factor, which already represents an annual citation benchmark.

Example: The paper’s recent rate is 5 citations per year and the two-year journal impact factor is 3.0. The reported value is 1.67: the paper’s recent annual rate is about 67% above that benchmark.

Reported valueWhat it means
Greater than 1.0The paper’s recent annual rate is above the stated benchmark.
About 1.0The paper’s recent annual rate is similar to the stated benchmark.
Less than 1.0The paper’s recent annual rate is below the stated benchmark.

If a different benchmark is used, replace “2y-IF” with its actual name—for example, Citations Per Year (Against 5y-IF) or Citations Per Year (Against Field Median).

What to Report

  • The citation source and the date checked.
  • The recent period used, such as the past 2 or 5 years.
  • Whether self-citations were included or excluded.
  • The exact name, year, value, and source of any benchmark.
  • Enough field, article-age, article-type, and database context to interpret the result responsibly.

Do not combine the three metrics into a single score or treat them as measures of research quality.
